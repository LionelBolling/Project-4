# Wordless story continuity

This is an implementation reference, never displayed in the game.

The playable character is the shadow from the first bedroom shot. It already
wears the boy's appearance. The real boy was imprisoned in the boombox before
the game began. No new body exchange happens during the ending.

1. The apparent boy enters the room without a mirror cutscene. Interacting
   with the mirror nearby can reveal the shadow once per playthrough. Consume
   the interaction immediately, and return control after the shot.
2. Turning on the boombox frames its cassette window. The real boy, keeping
   his original neon character design, is already inside and reaches outward.
   This shot establishes two figures before the playable character dreams.
3. Sleeping opens the connection into the tape. In the city, the captive boy
   pushes the pursuer away and runs through the recordings. His appearance
   stays consistent across the hub and every course. The player follows him.
4. The last tape displays a live view of the bedroom. The boy is attempting
   to reach home. The final confrontation stops that escape.
5. The controlled shadow leaves the boombox in the same borrowed appearance.
   Its true shape is briefly exposed, without depicting an exchange of bodies.
   The miniature boy remains in the cassette window during the pickup.
6. Destroying the boombox cuts immediately to the connecting portal collapsing
   inside. The shadow sighs with relief and returns to the stolen bed.
7. One static transition exposes the shadow's actual body for two seconds.
   It resumes the boy's appearance and laughs. This confirms who the player
   has controlled all along.
8. Return to the real boy and the sealed exit. His reaching gesture repeats
   the opening cassette shot. After waiting, he turns and walks away. Fade
   out and return to the title automatically.

Story information is carried by framing, recognizable character bodies,
repeated gestures, sound, and the portal. Do not add explanatory dialogue,
subtitles, written objectives, lore signs, or a last-minute body-swap scene.
Keyboard prompts and ordinary game menu controls are separate from narration.
Preserve the original character models, outfits, materials, and race portraits.
The real boy keeps his neon design throughout the story, including the ending.
